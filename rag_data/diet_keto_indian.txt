Keto and Indian Balanced Diets — Side-by-Side

Ketogenic Diet
The classic ketogenic diet derives ~70-75% of energy from fat, ~20% from protein, and only ~5-10% from carbohydrates. By keeping daily net carbs under 50 g (often under 20 g), the body shifts into ketosis, burning fatty acids and ketones for fuel.

Keto Allowed Foods
Eggs, fatty fish, meat, butter, ghee, full-fat cheese, paneer, cream, leafy greens, broccoli, cauliflower, zucchini, avocado, olives, nuts (almonds, pecans, macadamias), seeds, coconut, MCT oil, olive oil.

Keto Foods to Avoid
Sugar, honey, fruits (except berries in small amounts), wheat, rice, oats, lentils, chickpeas, potatoes, sweet potatoes, milk, sweetened yogurt.

Keto — Possible Benefits
Rapid initial weight loss, appetite suppression, better glycemic control in some Type 2 diabetes patients, useful adjunct in drug-resistant epilepsy.

Keto — Cautions
Long-term adherence is hard; can raise LDL cholesterol in some; risks of nutrient deficiencies (fiber, B vitamins, magnesium); not suitable for pregnancy, breastfeeding, kidney disease, or pancreatitis.

Indian Balanced Diet
A traditional Indian balanced diet (modeled on ICMR-NIN guidelines) provides ~50-55% energy from carbohydrates, 15-20% from protein, and 25-30% from fat. It centers on whole grains, dals, vegetables, dairy, and seasonal fruits.

Sample Indian Balanced Day
- Breakfast: 2 multigrain rotis or 1 bowl vegetable poha + 1 boiled egg + 1 cup unsweetened tea.
- Mid-morning: 1 fruit (apple, papaya, or orange).
- Lunch: 1 cup brown rice or 2 jowar rotis + 1 cup mixed dal + 1 katori sabzi + curd + salad.
- Snack: Roasted chana or sprouts chaat.
- Dinner: 2 rotis + paneer or chicken curry + sautéed greens + small bowl of curd.

Pros of Indian Balanced
Sustainable, nutrient-diverse, fiber-rich, easy on Indian taste palate, broadly safe.

Cons
Carbohydrate-heavy if portions are not controlled — common driver of insulin resistance in sedentary urban Indians.
