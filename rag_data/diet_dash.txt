DASH Diet — Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension

Purpose
The DASH diet was designed by the U.S. National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute to lower blood pressure without medication. It emphasizes low-sodium, potassium-rich foods and is widely recommended for hypertension, prehypertension, and general cardiovascular health.

Daily Targets (Standard 2,000 kcal Plan)
- Grains: 6-8 servings (prefer whole grains)
- Vegetables: 4-5 servings
- Fruits: 4-5 servings
- Fat-free or low-fat dairy: 2-3 servings
- Lean meat, poultry, fish: 6 oz or less
- Nuts, seeds, legumes: 4-5 servings per week
- Fats and oils: 2-3 servings (prefer plant-based)
- Sweets and added sugars: 5 or fewer servings per week
- Sodium: under 2,300 mg/day (ideal 1,500 mg/day)

Sample Day
- Breakfast: Oatmeal with sliced banana and walnuts; skim milk; a glass of orange juice.
- Lunch: Whole-grain wrap with roasted chicken, spinach, tomatoes, hummus; cucumber salad.
- Snack: Greek yogurt with berries.
- Dinner: Baked white fish with herbs, brown rice, steamed broccoli, mixed bean salad.

Sodium Reduction Strategies
- Cook from scratch; restaurant and packaged foods drive most sodium intake.
- Read labels — anything over 600 mg/serving is high.
- Use lemon juice, garlic, ginger, herbs, vinegar, and salt-free spice blends to flavor.
- Rinse canned beans and vegetables to remove brine.
- Skip processed meats (sausages, salami, ham, hot dogs).

Potassium Powerhouses
Bananas, sweet potatoes, spinach, beans, lentils, tomatoes, oranges, avocados, yogurt.

Indian Adaptations
- Use brown rice or millets (ragi, jowar) instead of polished rice.
- Reduce salt by ~30% while cooking; do not add salt at the table.
- Limit pickles, papads, ready-made chutneys, and packaged snacks.
- Choose tandoori or steamed preparations over deep-fried.
