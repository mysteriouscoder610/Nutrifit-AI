Anemia, Obesity, Celiac, IBS, Heart, and Kidney Disease — Nutrition Guides

Iron-Deficiency Anemia
- Eat iron-rich foods: red meat, liver, sardines, eggs, lentils, chickpeas, kidney beans, dark leafy greens, jaggery, dates, fortified cereals.
- Pair plant iron with vitamin C foods (citrus, tomato, capsicum, amla) to boost absorption 3-4x.
- Avoid tea and coffee with meals — tannins block iron absorption; wait at least an hour after eating.
- Cook in a cast-iron pan when possible.
- Take prescribed iron supplements with vitamin C, on an empty stomach if tolerated.

Obesity / Weight Management
- Create a sustainable 400-600 kcal/day deficit. Aim for 0.5 kg/week loss.
- 1.6-2.0 g protein per kg goal body weight protects muscle while losing fat.
- Fiber 30-40 g/day from vegetables, fruits, legumes, and whole grains keeps you full.
- Track for 2 weeks to learn portions; then transition to mindful eating.
- Combine dietary changes with 150-300 min/week aerobic + 2-3 strength sessions.
- Sleep 7-9 hours; short sleep raises ghrelin and increases hunger.

Celiac Disease
- Strict, lifelong gluten-free diet — no wheat, barley, rye, or contaminated oats.
- Safe staples: rice, millets (jowar, ragi, bajra), quinoa, buckwheat, amaranth, gluten-free oats, all dals/legumes, fresh vegetables, fruits, eggs, dairy, plain meats and fish.
- Watch for hidden gluten in soy sauce, asafoetida (hing — buy gluten-free), beer, sausages, ready meals, malt vinegar.
- Replace cross-contaminated kitchen tools (toaster, wooden boards, sieves) with dedicated GF versions.

IBS (Irritable Bowel Syndrome)
- Trial a low-FODMAP diet for 4-6 weeks (under a dietitian), then reintroduce systematically.
- High-FODMAP foods often triggering symptoms: onion, garlic, wheat, beans, milk, apples, pears, mango, mushrooms, cauliflower.
- Better-tolerated: rice, quinoa, lactose-free dairy, firm tofu, eggs, lean meat, carrot, zucchini, cucumber, spinach, oranges, kiwi, strawberries.
- Eat regular, smaller meals; chew thoroughly; manage stress (gut-brain axis is real).
- Increase soluble fiber slowly (oats, psyllium); reduce caffeine and alcohol.

Heart Disease
- Mediterranean or DASH pattern; replace saturated and trans fats with olive oil, nuts, fatty fish.
- Saturated fat under 7% of daily calories; trans fat as close to zero as possible.
- 25-30 g fiber daily; soluble fiber (oats, beans, psyllium) lowers LDL.
- Limit sodium to 1,500-2,300 mg/day.
- Plant sterols (2 g/day from fortified foods or supplements) modestly lower LDL.
- Two servings of fatty fish per week or 1-2 g EPA+DHA from a quality supplement.

Kidney Disease (CKD Stages 3-5, non-dialysis)
- Moderate protein: 0.6-0.8 g/kg body weight to slow progression.
- Limit sodium under 2,000 mg/day; potassium and phosphorus per labs.
- Choose lower-potassium fruits: apple, pear, berries, grapes; avoid bananas, oranges, potatoes if potassium is high.
- Avoid added phosphorus in cola, processed meats, packaged foods (read for "phos-" ingredients).
- Stay well-hydrated unless restricted by your nephrologist.
- Adjust per blood work in consultation with your nephrologist and renal dietitian.

General Note
These guidelines are educational. Always personalize with a clinician and registered dietitian who can review labs, medications, and individual context.
