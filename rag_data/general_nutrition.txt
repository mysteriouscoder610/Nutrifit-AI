General Nutrition Guidelines (ICMR / WHO synthesis)

Macronutrient Distribution
- Carbohydrates 45-65% of daily energy, prefer whole, fiber-rich sources.
- Protein 10-20% (typically 0.8-1.2 g/kg for sedentary adults; 1.6-2.2 g/kg for active people, athletes, or anyone doing structured strength training).
- Fats 20-35%, mostly mono- and polyunsaturated; saturated fats under 10%; trans fats avoided.

Fiber
25-30 g/day for women, 30-40 g/day for men. Sources: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds. Increase gradually with adequate water.

Hydration
30-35 ml per kg body weight per day from fluids and water-rich foods. More with hot weather, sweating, fever, lactation.

Salt and Sugar
- Salt under 5 g/day (about 1 teaspoon, ~2,000 mg sodium).
- Added sugar under 10% of energy; ideally under 5%.

Key Micronutrients (and Indian deficiency hotspots)
- Iron: most adult women in India are deficient; pair plant iron with vitamin C.
- Vitamin B12: vegans/vegetarians should supplement 250-1,000 mcg/week.
- Vitamin D: very common deficiency; 20 minutes midday sun + supplements if 25(OH)D < 30 ng/mL.
- Calcium: 1,000-1,200 mg/day from dairy, fortified plant milks, ragi, sesame, almonds.
- Iodine: use iodized salt; do not over-restrict salt without ensuring iodine intake.
- Omega-3 (EPA/DHA): fatty fish twice weekly or algae-based supplement for vegetarians.

Meal Timing
- Eat within an hour of waking to stabilize cortisol and morning hunger.
- Aim for 3 meals + 1-2 snacks; protein at every meal.
- Stop eating 2-3 hours before sleep for better sleep quality and lower reflux.

Plate Method (No Counting)
- 1/2 plate non-starchy vegetables.
- 1/4 plate lean protein.
- 1/4 plate whole grains or starchy vegetables.
- 1 thumb of healthy fat (olive oil, nuts, ghee, avocado).

Common Myths Debunked
- "Carbs make you fat." False — excess calories do; fiber-rich carbs are essential.
- "Fat is dangerous." False — healthy fats are protective; trans fats are not.
- "Skipping breakfast helps lose weight." Mixed evidence; depends on total intake and consistency.
- "Detox juices clear toxins." False — your liver and kidneys do that work; juice cleanses spike sugar.
- "Protein damages kidneys." False in healthy people; relevant only in pre-existing kidney disease.
